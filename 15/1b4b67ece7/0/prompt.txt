### 2. The Hyper-Critical Flaws (Where Reviewers will tear it apart)

A sharp reviewer will absolutely crucify this paper on four specific fronts:

#### A. The Procedural Fatal Flaw: "Running" Experiments
*   **The Attack:** In the Abstract and Section 4, the authors confidently claim: *"Crucially, standard MoE rescue mechanisms... all fail to rescue hidden-state routing."* Yet, looking at **Table 3**, 8 out of the 11 mechanisms are explicitly marked as `"partial*"` or `"running"`.
*   **The Reality:** Submitting incomplete primary tables is an egregious violation of top-tier peer-review standards. You cannot assert a definitive negative result when 75% of your SLURM jobs haven't finished. If Expert-Choice routing or Z-loss *does* fix the collapse in the pending runs, the paper’s central premise is threatened. This is grounds for an automatic rejection if not instantly resolved in the rebuttal.

#### B. The "Frozen Paradox" is a Fabricated Fallacy
The authors heavily market the "Frozen Paradox," claiming that freezing the backbone improves performance because *"unfreezing buys routing collapse, not expressivity—reversing the standard PEFT heuristic."* A critical reviewer will easily dismantle this logic:
1.  **Factually Contradicted by their own Tables:** On the `Weather` dataset (Table 1), Frozen RR-MoA scores **0.289**, while Unfrozen (Last-2) RR-MoA scores **0.251** (a 13% *improvement*). Unfreezing explicitly buys expressivity here, completely invalidating the claim that frozen is always better.
2.  **Logically Contradictory:** The authors state that RR-MoA routes on raw inputs, making it *"immune to gradient co-adaptation."* If RR-MoA is immune to collapse, then Unfrozen RR-MoA cannot be suffering from routing collapse. The performance drop on ETTh1 is just standard small-dataset overfitting.
3.  **Apples-to-Oranges Fallacy:** The authors claim "Frozen RR-MoA beats full fine-tuning... reversing the standard PEFT heuristic." But Full Fine-Tuning is a *single-head* adapter. Frozen RR-MoA is a *Mixture of Experts*. Comparing a Frozen MoE to an Unfrozen Single-Head and concluding "unfreezing is bad" is false logic. For single heads, unfreezing *improves* MSE (1.254 down to 1.060, Table 4). The paradox is entirely manufactured.

#### C. The System-Level Motivation is Mathematically Backwards
*   **The Attack:** To justify using a frozen TSFM over `DLinear` (which beats them from scratch), the authors argue that for N=100 tenants, per-tenant `DLinear` requires too many training pipelines, whereas adapter-swapping a TSFM uses *"45x less memory than per-tenant model copies."*
*   **The Reality:** `DLinear` has 49K parameters. The RR-MoA adapter has 426K parameters, plus the 35M parameter TSFM backbone. Assuming float32 (4 bytes):
    *   100 tenants * 49K `DLinear` = **~19.6 MB**.
    *   1 shared TSFM + 100 RR-MoA adapters = 140 MB + (100 * 1.7 MB) = **~310 MB**.
    `DLinear` is 15x *smaller* in memory, trains in seconds, and gets better MSE. The multi-tenant efficiency argument for TSFMs over `DLinear` is mathematically absurd. The only valid motivation remaining for the TSFM is cross-task reuse (imputation/zero-shot).

#### D. The "Dual-Stream" Trojan Horse (Is the TSFM dead weight?)
*   **The Attack:** To finally beat `DLinear`, the authors introduce "Dual-Stream" experts that blend the TSFM backbone features with an MLP operating directly on the raw input $X_{raw}$. 
*   **The Missing Baseline:** In Appendix 26, the authors explicitly prove that the TSFM encoder *destroys* non-linear temporal structure. If the TSFM destroys the signal, and the dual-stream fix requires raw data to recover it, isn't the raw-data MLP doing 99% of the heavy lifting? The paper desperately needs an ablation showing a Mixture of Adapters operating **ONLY on raw MLPs** (deleting the 35M-parameter TSFM entirely). If a pure Raw-MLP MoE matches the Dual-Stream performance, then the massive Foundation Model is exposed as nothing more than a computationally expensive, redundant ensemble member.
