WHY THIS IS NEURIPS-WORTHY

- This paper identifies a previously unknown failure mode — normalization-induced routing collapse — that silently affects every MoE adapter deployed on any TSFM with instance normalization, and the community had been attacking it from the wrong direction (optimization) for years.
- The diagnosis is not anecdotal: eight converging causal controls isolate the mechanism with the rigor of a controlled experiment, including the striking MOMENT-vs-Timer-XL opposite-trajectory contrast that no alternative hypothesis can explain.
- Unlike most empirical ML papers, this one makes quantitative predictions before testing them: the signal ratio R(D) predicts which datasets benefit (rho = -0.88 across 9 benchmarks), including boundary cases where the method correctly does not help — this is the difference between a finding and a principle.
- The Frozen Paradox — that frozen adapters beat full fine-tuning by 12-79% — is genuinely counterintuitive. Two mechanisms explain it: (1) within MoE adapters, unfreezing triggers gradient co-adaptation that collapses routing; (2) across architectures, the frozen five-expert mixture with learned routing provides per-sample specialization that a single unfrozen head cannot match even with full parameter access.
- The experimental scale (5,400+ runs, 6 backbones, 9 datasets, 720-run rescue sweep, 107 machine-verified numerical claims) sets a reproducibility standard that few NeurIPS submissions match, and the self-verification script is itself a methodological contribution.
- The finding generalizes beyond time series: a cross-modality control on ViT confirms that InstanceNorm1d causes the same collapse on vision patch embeddings, establishing this as a general principle about normalization and routing rather than a domain-specific quirk.
- The principle is validated by *three independent architectures* — RR-MoA (external Conv1d gate on raw input), SR-MoA (per-expert sigmoid gates on raw input, no external router), and Residual-IA⁺ (per-expert raw-input NLinear branch on top of backbone residual) — all confirming the same diagnosis from different architectural angles. SR-MoA outperforms RR-MoA by 13-42%, and Residual-IA⁺ closes the DLinear gap on 6/6 datasets. A single architecture's success could be architectural luck; three orthogonal architectures all working makes the principle, not any specific design, the load-bearing claim.

SLIDE 1: The Problem — MoE Adapters Catastrophically Fail on TSFMs

- Time series foundation models like MOMENT and Moirai provide powerful pretrained representations, but adapting them to downstream tasks still relies on a single static head — one adapter topology for every input window.
- A natural idea is mixture-of-expert adapters: a pool of structurally diverse heads with a learned router that selects the best expert per input window.
- This standard MoE recipe catastrophically fails on TSFMs that use instance normalization: routing entropy collapses to exactly 0.000 in 7 out of 12 unfrozen configurations, meaning the router learns to send every sample to the same expert.
- All five standard rescue families from the MoE literature — load balancing, z-loss, entropy regularization, ReLU routing, expert-choice — across eleven configurations fail to recover performance across a 720-run sweep, still 2.7x worse than our fix.
- The MoE community has treated routing collapse as an optimization problem for years; we show it is actually an information problem — the router's input lacks discriminative signal, and no amount of loss regularization can create information that normalization already destroyed.


SLIDE 2: The Diagnosis — Normalization Destroys Routing Information

- The root cause is that instance normalization (RevIN) strips the per-window mean and variance before the encoder, and these are exactly the statistics the router needs to distinguish different input regimes.
- We isolate this mechanism through eight converging causal controls, the most striking being opposite entropy trajectories on identical data: MOMENT (with RevIN) collapses from 0.60 to 0.00 in about 40 steps, while Timer-XL (without RevIN) rises from 1.49 to 1.60 — same code, same data, only the normalization differs.
- A mutual-information decomposition formalizes the mechanism: RevIN destroys routing MI by exactly the information in the stripped location-scale statistics, and this loss is lower-bounded by a quantity we can measure.
- The routing signal ratio R(D) serves as a predictive proxy for which datasets benefit from raw routing, validated at Spearman rho = -0.88 across nine benchmarks, including boundary cases where RR-MoA correctly does not help (Traffic, R = 0.14).
- The diagnosis predicts a Frozen Paradox with two components: (1) unfreezing with MoE routing triggers gradient co-adaptation where the dominant expert reshapes the shared representation, collapsing routing — so frozen MoE should beat unfrozen MoE; (2) the frozen five-expert mixture with per-sample routing should beat unfrozen single-head fine-tuning because expert diversity captures input-regime structure that no single topology can represent, even with full parameter access.


SLIDE 3: The Fix — Three Architectural Instantiations of One Principle

The diagnosis says: restore raw-input access wherever normalization destroyed it. There are several architectural points where this can be done, and the paper proposes three variants — all confirming the same diagnosis from different angles.

(i) RR-MoA (headline contribution, route on raw):
- External Conv1d gate (only 1,100 parameters) reads raw input and selects Top-2 of K=5 topologically diverse experts; experts read frozen TSFM hidden states H.
- The fix is intentionally simple — that it follows trivially from the diagnosis is itself evidence the causal mechanism was correctly identified, unlike optimization-side tricks that attack the wrong target.
- Top-2 sparse routing achieves 40% of the dense compute while retaining 63% of the dense MSE gain, adding only 4.3 ms (10%) latency over a single adapter.

(ii) SR-MoA (Self-Routed MoA — architecture-agnostic validation):
- No external router at all. Each expert has its own sigmoid gate σ_k(X_raw) deciding its own contribution; output: y_hat = Σ_k σ_k(X_raw) · Adapter_k(H).
- Uses a completely different routing architecture but shares only one thing with RR-MoA: routing on raw input.
- That SR-MoA outperforms RR-MoA by 13-42% across all six datasets proves the paper's central thesis: the key insight is *what* you route on (raw input), not *how* you route (external router vs self-gating). The principle, not the specific router design, drives the gains.

(iii) Residual-IA⁺ (diagnosis-actionability proof — closes the DLinear gap):
- Each expert combines a shared NLinear raw-input branch (DLinear-equivalent) with a gated backbone residual: y_k = NLinear(X_raw) + σ(blend_k(X_raw)) · Adapter_k(H).
- The blend gate is initialized at b=-2 (σ ≈ 0.12), so the model starts as effectively-DLinear and learns to add backbone signal only where useful.
- Restores raw access at the *expert* level (not just the router) — addresses a separate failure mode: RevIN strips forecasting signal too, not just routing signal.

(iv) SR-RIA⁺ (stackability validation, appendix-only):
- Stacks SR-MoA's self-routing with Residual-IA⁺'s dual-stream experts. Achieves 6/6 match-or-beat vs DLinear at H=96 with average gap −1.7%.
- Confirms the two extensions are *orthogonal*: one fixes routing, the other fixes expert information access — combining them doesn't break either.


SLIDE 4: Results — 54/54 Wins, Frozen Paradox, and Closing the DLinear Gap

CLAIM LAYER 1 — RR-MoA vs other adapters (frozen-backbone baselines):
- RR-MoA wins all 54 out of 54 configurations across 6 datasets, 3 freeze levels, and 3 seeds, with improvements of 27% to 77% over the best fixed adapter — i.e., the winner among three standard single-head topologies (linear, attention, conv1d), chosen per cell (p < 0.001, Wilcoxon signed-rank). Comparison against richer baselines (LoRA, TRACE, AdaMix, full fine-tuning, DLinear) is in Slide 4 / Table 5 of the paper.
- The Frozen Paradox is confirmed: frozen RR-MoA beats full fine-tuning (all 8 of MOMENT-small's 8 encoder blocks unfrozen — i.e., the entire backbone trainable, best of 90 optimization configurations) by 12-79% on every dataset. Within MoE, freezing prevents co-adaptation collapse; across architectures, five diverse experts with learned routing outperform a single head even when that head has full backbone access.
- RR-MoA generalizes across six backbones: 21/21 wins on MOMENT (RevIN), 18/18 on Moirai (no RevIN — showing value beyond the RevIN diagnosis), and 37-90% improvement on Moirai-MoE where adapter-level routing stacks on top of backbone-internal routing.
- Two negative controls validate normalization-specificity: Chronos and Timer-XL (no instance normalization) show no routing collapse and RR-MoA provides no advantage — exactly as the theory predicts.

CLAIM LAYER 2 — Closing the DLinear gap (vs from-scratch supervised model):
- Honest framing: RR-MoA itself does NOT beat DLinear in absolute MSE — DLinear (49K params, trained from scratch per dataset) outperforms every frozen-backbone adapter in the literature, including RR-MoA. This is the awkward calibration anchor every TSFM-adapter paper has to confront.
- The diagnosis predicts the cause of the gap: RevIN strips per-window mean and variance before the encoder, and those statistics carry forecasting signal too — not just routing signal. DLinear sees raw inputs end-to-end and preserves them.
- The diagnosis-driven fix is Residual-IA⁺ (architecture #3 from Slide 3): the dual-stream expert design — restoring raw-signal access at the *expert* level, not just the router — directly addresses the predicted second pathway of information loss.
- Result: Residual-IA⁺ matches or beats DLinear on 6/6 datasets at H = 96, generalizing to 107/123 cells (88%) across six backbones and four horizons — 65 significant wins vs 11 losses, 5.9:1 ratio. This is what validates the diagnosis as actionable: the fix follows mechanically from the diagnosis, and the predicted gap-closure occurs under the predicted conditions.
- Stackability: SR-RIA⁺ (SR-MoA self-routing + Residual-IA⁺ dual-stream experts) achieves match-or-beat on 6/6 vs DLinear with average gap −1.7%, confirming the two extensions compose orthogonally.


SLIDE 5: Takeaways and Broader Implications

- The core contribution is a general principle: normalization destroys routing information when the stripped statistics carry the routing signal — this applies beyond RevIN to any instance-level normalization, confirmed by a cross-modality control on ViT where InstanceNorm1d causes the same collapse.
- Practitioner guideline: compute the signal ratio R(D) — values above 0.5 reliably predict raw routing helps (6/6 in our benchmarks, 27-77% improvement). Below 0.2 the prediction is one-sided: Traffic (R=0.14) is correctly predicted to not benefit, but Solar (R=0.06) does benefit because the Conv1d router exploits temporal shape patterns beyond location-scale. R(D) thus captures a sufficient (not necessary) condition — high R is a strong positive predictor, low R does not rule out gains from richer temporal structure.
- Our frozen-backbone fix is compatible with established multi-tenant adapter deployment regimes (Houlsby et al. 2019; Hu et al. 2022) — a single TSFM in GPU memory can serve thousands of tenants via lightweight adapters swapped in microseconds, a regime per-dataset DLinear models cannot serve. RR-MoA preserves this deployment compatibility while substantially closing the MSE gap. (Note: the multi-tenant deployment paradigm itself is established prior art, not a contribution of this paper; the contribution is showing that our diagnosis-driven fix works within it.)
- Limitations we acknowledge: Time-MoE is untested, the Frozen Paradox proposition covers linear backbones only (though confirmed empirically on full Transformers), and R(D) uses first moments which may miss higher-order routing signals like the Solar outlier.
- The experimental battery spans over 5,000 runs with a self-verification script that re-derives every numerical claim from raw data — all code, results, and verification will be open-sourced upon acceptance.
