## **Response to Reviewer Pm4m**

We thank the reviewer for the careful follow-up. Placing the two Moirai statements side by side exposed one incorrect sentence in our previous response, and the proposed NLinear baseline was indeed the right control for separating level anchoring from the rest of Residual-IA+. We report the correction and the new control directly below.

### **1. Moirai: §4.1 is correct; one sentence in our previous W3 response was not**

The statement in §4.1 is correct: Moirai’s non-learnable I/O scaling preserves the relevant location-scale information in the hidden representation available to the router. Our previous W3 response incorrectly stated that Moirai “strips per-window location-scale from the hidden states.” We had described what normalization does to its immediate input as though it were also true of the router-visible representation. We thank the reviewer for identifying the contradiction, and we will remove that sentence.

We have now tested the issue directly. We took the exact hidden-state tensor consumed by the router and trained a probe to recover each input window’s mean (M):

* **Moirai:** (R^2=0.996)
* **MOMENT with RevIN:** (R^2=-0.311)
* **MOMENT with RevIN disabled:** (R^2=0.999)

The mean probe is sufficient to adjudicate the contradiction: if Moirai had stripped per-window location information as our previous response claimed, (M) would not be recoverable. Instead, it is almost perfectly recoverable. Conversely, RevIN removes that information from MOMENT’s router-visible hidden states, and disabling RevIN restores it. This directly supports the distinction made in §4.1.

The Moirai gain must therefore be interpreted more narrowly than in our previous response. Moirai does not collapse, and raw routing is not repairing missing location-scale information. However, the gain also cannot be attributed to the backbone-free **Pure Raw-MLP** mechanism from Appendix P.3: in RR-MoA, the raw window is supplied only to the router, while every expert continues to consume frozen backbone representations. Architecturally, the difference is therefore in the routing path, not in a raw-input prediction branch.

The supported conclusion is consequently:

> On Moirai, raw input is a more effective router input even though the hidden representation retains the window mean; the present experiment does not establish which additional routing cues or inductive biases cause that advantage.

Nothing has to be absent from the hidden state for changing the router input to improve routing. Nevertheless, we agree that the Moirai result should be presented as a separate routing-input benefit, not as collapse repair or recovery of missing ((M,\Sigma)). It is not needed as evidence for the collapse mechanism.

The reviewer is also correct that the rule we stated to Reviewer 8b2Z was incomplete. We stated the information condition but omitted the co-adaptation condition from Proposition 1. The complete criterion is:

1. routing-relevant information is attenuated or removed from the representation read by the router; **and**
2. a learnable affine component can co-adapt with the router and amplify the resulting imbalance.

Moirai satisfies neither condition: the window mean remains recoverable from the hidden states, and its I/O scaling is non-learnable. Thus, §4.1 and Proposition 1 remain consistent; what was incorrect was our explanation of the separate Moirai performance gain. We will revise §4.1, Table N.2’s discussion, and the cross-normalizer rule so that this distinction is explicit throughout.

1. NLinear-style level anchoring versus the DLinear anchor; and
2. the contribution of the remaining Residual-IA+ architecture, including its backbone path.

\hat{y}=\mathrm{Linear}(x-x_T)+x_T.

The control confirms the reviewer’s explanation on the datasets where level drift matters. Across nine datasets, NLinear is stronger than DLinear on three:

* **Exchange:** 28.9% lower MSE
* **ETTh2:** 3.8% lower at (H=96), and 19.0% lower at (H=192)
* **ETTm2:** 7.9% lower

On the remaining six datasets, NLinear provides no corresponding advantage and is equal to or worse than DLinear. Thus, the relative strength of the two linear anchors is dataset-dependent rather than uniformly in favor of either one.
