Frozen RR-MoA also beats full fine-tuning by 12–79% (the Frozen Paradox); two architecturally distinct variants confirm the principle generalizes beyond this specific router.

Contribution Type: Use-inspired: The main contribution is in framing or designing approaches to meet the needs of a specific real-world application. (This often involves, e.g., engaging with domain experts.)
Strengths And Weaknesses:
Strengths The experimental evaluation covers six representative long-term time-series forecasting (LTSF) benchmarks, providing evidence across a diverse set of datasets.

The paper identifies a plausible causal mechanism, termed normalization-induced routing collapse, and proposes a simple yet targeted intervention that directly addresses the issue without introducing substantial architectural complexity.

The proposed signal-relation metric appears capable of predicting dataset vulnerability prior to training, which could provide practical value beyond the specific method introduced in the paper.

The experiments provide empirical evidence for the existence of routing collapse and demonstrate the effectiveness of RR-MoA in mitigating this phenomenon.

Weaknesses Since the router operates directly on raw inputs, bypassing input normalization, the approach may be less memory-efficient than conventional designs. The implications for GPU memory usage and computational efficiency are not fully discussed.

The theoretical analysis focuses primarily on the interaction between instance normalization and router inputs. It remains unclear to what extent the proposed explanation accounts for other factors, such as dataset-specific distributional properties or feature asymmetries.

The scalability of the approach deserves further investigation. As the number of experts increases, the dimensionality of the router input and the associated memory bandwidth requirements may become a bottleneck.

The causal analysis is conducted mainly through interventions on instance normalization. It is therefore unclear whether similar routing-collapse phenomena arise under other normalization schemes, such as BatchNorm, LayerNorm, or RMSNorm.

The experimental evaluation is limited to forecasting and missing-value imputation tasks. The applicability of the proposed insights to other settings, such as classification or generative modeling, remains unexplored.

Some experiments appear to use a relatively small number of training epochs. It would be helpful to provide additional evidence that the reported results are not affected by incomplete convergence.

The statistical analysis may be sensitive to the choice of weighting schemes and multiple-testing corrections. If these procedures are overly conservative, they could reduce statistical power and potentially obscure meaningful effects.

Quality: 2: not good
Clarity: 2: not good
Significance: 2: not good
Originality: 3: good
Questions:
The paper attributes routing collapse primarily to the use of normalized inputs for routing decisions. Have the authors considered applying the proposed raw-routing strategy to existing MoE architectures, such as AdaMix or related variants? Such an experiment would help clarify whether the routing input itself is the dominant source of the problem, or whether other architectural factors also contribute to routing collapse.

The causal analysis is based on eight intervention settings, including RevIN ablation and BatchNorm swap. Do the authors expect the same causal mechanisms and intervention effects to hold in other tasks or domains, such as image modeling? Additional discussion on the generality of the proposed explanation would help assess whether the identified mechanism is specific to LTSF or reflects a broader phenomenon in MoE architectures.

Several experiments appear to be conducted using relatively short training schedules. Have the authors evaluated the method under longer training regimes (200 epochs or beyond)? It would be useful to understand whether the reported advantages persist after full convergence and whether longer training affects routing stability or performance.

Limitations:
The empirical analysis focuses primarily on instance-normalization methods that remove sample-specific statistics, such as RevIN and RMSNorm. It remains unclear whether similar routing-collapse phenomena would arise under other normalization schemes (BatchNorm or GroupNorm), which may limit the generality of the proposed explanation. The experiments consider forecasting horizons up to 720 time steps. It is therefore unclear whether the same conclusions hold for substantially longer temporal horizons, where internal representations and routing behaviors may become more diverse. The study employs a fixed set of relatively simple adapters (linear, attention-based, and convolutional adapters). The behavior of the proposed method in settings with learned, dynamically generated, or substantially larger adapter pools remains unexplored. Consequently, the scalability and robustness of the conclusions to more complex routing scenarios are not yet established.
