### ⚠️ CRITICAL Vulnerabilities (Where "Reviewer 2" Will Attack)

To secure a unanimous Accept, you **must** fix the following issues—especially a fatal contradiction in the abstract.

**1. FATAL FLAW: The "54/54 Wins" Abstract Contradiction**
*   **The Issue:** In your abstract, you write: *"RR-MoA achieves 54/54 wins (p<0.001, Wilcoxon) across six backbones including two negative controls..."* However, in Section 4.2 (Cross-backbone generalization), you explicitly state that for the negative controls (Chronos, Timer-XL), *"RR-MoA provides no advantage (4/18 wins, within chance)."* 
*   **The Fix:** A sharp reviewer will instantly catch this and accuse you of overclaiming or faking data in the abstract. Based on Table 1, the 54/54 wins refer to **6 datasets $\times$ 3 freeze levels $\times$ 3 seeds** on the primary backbone. You accidentally wrote "six backbones" instead of "six datasets" in the abstract. Change the abstract to: *"RR-MoA achieves 54/54 wins across our primary grid... On two negative controls without instance normalization, RR-MoA provides no advantage, exactly as our theory predicts."*

**2. The Rushed Pacing of "Residual-IA+"**
*   **The Issue:** You spend five pages building up base RR-MoA, only to reveal in Table 5 that it is still +57% worse than DLinear. To fix this, you suddenly introduce "dual-stream", "multi-resolution", and "Residual-IA+" in a single dense paragraph at the very end of Section 4.2. A critical reviewer will view this as a rushed "bag of tricks" crammed in at the last minute just to beat the linear baseline.
*   **The Fix:** Elevate Residual-IA+ into **Section 3 (Method)**. Present baseline RR-MoA in Section 3.2, and immediately follow it with a short Section 3.3 explaining Residual-IA+. Frame it not as an afterthought, but as the ultimate, finalized architectural instantiation of raw-signal routing.

**3. "Why Use a Foundation Model at All?"**
*   **The Issue:** If a tiny 49K-parameter DLinear model achieves 0.416 on ETTh1, and your complex MoE-adapted TSFM achieves 0.428 (with Residual-IA+), a reviewer will inevitably ask: *"Why are we paying the massive inference cost of a foundation model for equal/worse performance?"*
*   **The Defense:** You briefly touch on this in the Intro ("multi-tenant cloud serving... cross-task reuse"). You must lean into this harder when discussing Table 5. Remind the reviewer that DLinear *must* be trained from scratch per dataset, whereas a frozen TSFM can serve thousands of tasks simultaneously and handle zero-shot imputation/classification.

Just ensure the text introducing Proposition 1 explicitly states that it is a *linear motivation* for a phenomenon that empirically extends to deep networks.
