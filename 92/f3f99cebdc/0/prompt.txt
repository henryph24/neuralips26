use gemini nano banana pro to visualise the problem: "Time series foundation models like MOMENT and Moirai are powerful pretrained
representations. But the standard way to adapt them is to attach a single static head — one
adapter topology for every input window, no matter what the data looks like.
A natural improvement is mixture-of-expert adapters: a pool of structurally diverse heads with a
learned router that picks the best expert per window. AdaMix and Switch Transformer have
shown this works well in NLP.
Here is where things go sideways. On TSFMs that use instance normalization — and most of
the major ones do — this standard MoE recipe catastrophically fails. Routing entropy
collapses to exactly 0.000 in 7 of 12 unfrozen configurations. The router learns to send
every single sample to the same expert. The other four experts are dead weight.
Our first instinct was: this is a known optimization issue. So we tried all five standard rescue
families from the MoE literature — load balancing from Switch, z-loss from ST-MoE, entropy
regularization, ReLU routing, expert-choice. Eleven configurations across a 720-run sweep.
Zero of eleven recover. Still 2.7× worse than what we’ll show next.
Our claim — and the rest of this talk defends it — is that the MoE field has been treating this as
an optimization problem. We show it’s actually an information problem"
