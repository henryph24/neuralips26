Summary:
This paper studies why mixture of experts adapter heads can fail when adapting frozen time series foundation models. The authors identify normalization induced routing collapse, where a hidden state router collapses to a single expert within a few optimization steps when used with backbones that apply learnable affine instance normalization, such as RevIN in MOMENT. The paper argues that this failure is caused mainly by the router input rather than by the routing optimization itself, since RevIN removes per window mean and scale information that is important for distinguishing temporal regimes. This diagnosis is supported by a mutual information analysis, a training free signal ratio that correlates with collapse risk, and several causal controls, including normalization ablations, comparisons across backbones, and a replication in vision models. As a remedy, the authors propose RR-MoA, which routes using the raw input before normalization while keeping expert adaptation on frozen hidden states. The method is reported to outperform fixed adapters, LoRA, TRACE, AdaMix, and full fine tuning across all evaluated settings, with additional variants used to support generality across multiple backbones, forecasting horizons, and an imputation task.

TL;DR: MoE adapters on instance-normalized time series foundation models collapse; eight causal controls isolate RevIN as the cause and prescribe raw-input routing (RR-MoA/SR-MoA/Residual-IA+), beating prior adapters and full fine-tuning.
Abstract:
Time series foundation models (TSFMs) commonly adapt to new data by attaching a single trainable head to a frozen backbone, a one-size-fits-all setup that underfits heterogeneous regimes. Replacing the head with a mixture of experts is the standard upgrade, but on instance-normalized backbones (the dominant TSFM design class) it fails: routing entropy collapses to zero and one expert absorbs every input, a failure we call normalization-induced routing collapse. Standard MoE rescue mechanisms do not repair it, because the cause is in the router's input, not its optimization. Pre-encoder normalization strips the statistics a router would need to tell regimes apart. The authors identify normalization induced routing collapse, where a hidden state router collapses to a single expert within a few optimization steps when used with backbones that apply learnable affine instance normalization, such as RevIN in MOMENT. The paper argues that this failure is caused mainly by the router input rather than by the routing optimization itself, since RevIN removes per window mean and scale information that is important for distinguishing temporal regimes.
