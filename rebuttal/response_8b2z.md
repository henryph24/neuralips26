**Response to Reviewer 8b2Z**

We sincerely thank the reviewer for the constructive and careful assessment, and for recognizing the practical value of the signal-ratio metric. We are grateful for every question raised. Several are addressed by experiments in the paper that we will bring forward more prominently in the revision (memory, expert scaling, other normalizers, applying raw routing to an existing MoE, a vision replication), and where a concern called for something new (an interventional LayerNorm swap, 200-epoch convergence, a significance re-analysis), we ran it and tag it [NEW] below. We address each point in turn.

**W1 (memory of raw routing).** We can see why "routing on raw inputs" sounds memory-heavy; in practice the footprint is small. The router reads a single univariate length-L window and is an 853-parameter Conv1d gate (0.002% of the ~40M backbone, 57× smaller than a 49K DLinear), adding under 0.1 ms to the backbone's 52.9 ms forward pass. Total overhead across all three variants is +5.9% latency and +6 MB on a 359 MB backbone (Table B.1); peak memory is set by the frozen backbone every adapter method already loads.

**W2 (dataset-distributional / feature-asymmetry factors).** A fair ask. R(D) is in fact a dataset-distributional quantity, computed from raw statistics and predicting per-dataset vulnerability (ρ=-0.88, n=9), with Traffic (R=0.14) and Solar (R=0.06) the boundary/outlier cases. Appendix D.1 broadens the account with two standard information-theoretic views. The rate-distortion view treats instance normalization as lossy compression that deliberately discards each window's mean and scale, so R(D) measures how much of the routing signal lives in exactly that discarded part. The information-bottleneck view explains why collapse happens at all: the normalize-then-encode pipeline is trained to keep only what predicts the target and drop the rest, and "the rest" includes what the router needs. We agree that feature asymmetries beyond mean and scale are not yet formalized. Our own ablations already show that what the router actually relies on is broader than mean and standard deviation (μ,σ) (App. F, SSR +48%), and formalizing that broader signal is valuable future theory.

**W3 (scaling experts → router-input dimensionality / bandwidth).** This is a natural worry, since in a standard MoE the router input does grow with the expert count. In our design it does not: The router input is the raw window and is independent of K; only the K-way logit layer (64→K) grows, which is negligible. Because routing is Top-2, exactly two experts execute per sample regardless of K, so both active compute and memory traffic are O(2), not O(K). Table I.2 (K∈{1..10}) is the empirical stability check (within ±10% for K≥2). Expert count therefore creates no router-input or bandwidth bottleneck by construction; larger learned or dynamically-generated pools beyond K=10 are future work.

**W4 (other normalizers: BatchNorm / LayerNorm / RMSNorm).** This is a natural question, and the submitted App. H already tests it interventionally: swapping RevIN for BatchNorm1d (entropy 0.62→0.004) and GroupNorm (0.51→0.000) yields identical collapse, while removing normalization keeps it healthy (0.82). [NEW] To match this rigor for LayerNorm, we also swap a LayerNorm into MOMENT's input-normalization position (same App. H protocol): it collapses identically (entropy → 0.000), with the no-normalization control healthy at 0.825. The apparent tension with our LayerNorm/RMSNorm negative controls (Chronos, Timer-XL) is resolved by where the norm sits: at the input position it strips per-window statistics and collapses; encoder-internal LayerNorm/RMSNorm do not strip the router's input and do not collapse. The rule is whether the normalizer strips routing-relevant statistics at the router's input.

**W5 (only forecasting / imputation).** Beyond forecasting on six datasets and imputation (Table J, 7/8 wins), [NEW] a classification mechanism-check on three UEA datasets shows routing entropy stays healthy (1.46-1.59, no collapse), so the anti-collapse mechanism is not forecasting-specific; we scope this to mechanism-generalization since frozen features cap absolute accuracy. [NEW] An input-length ablation (L∈{96,192,336,512}, 72 runs) shows no collapse at any L and raw beats normalized routing at all 24 cells. Anomaly detection remains honest future work.

**W6 / Q3 (few epochs / convergence).** The complex dual-stream Residual-IA+ reaches its validation optimum early and overfits by 50 epochs; the simpler RR-MoA is robust to long training. Extending full fine-tuning to 50 and 100 epochs (90-config sweep) still loses to 15-epoch frozen RR-MoA by 33-71% (App. O). [NEW] We train RR-MoA to 200 epochs on ETTh1, ETTm1, Weather and Electricity (the dataset flagged as slowest to converge; 3 seeds each): routing never collapses (seed-mean entropy 1.02-1.34, minimum 0.87 across all 12 runs), and MSE is stable-to-improved versus 15 epochs (ETTh1 0.680→0.646, ETTm1 0.564→0.493). The reported 15-epoch numbers are conservative, not a convergence artifact.

**W7 (statistical corrections may be too conservative).** You are right that an over-conservative correction risks false negatives; we address both directions. [NEW] (i) Our claimed effect is correction-immaterial: the pooled Wilcoxon signed-rank on the RR-MoA-vs-best-fixed cells gives p ≈ 3×10⁻¹¹ uncorrected, so Bonferroni over every comparison in the paper still leaves it below 10⁻⁹, and Holm/BH only lower it further. A per-dataset Wilcoxon with 3-7 seeds is limited by seed count, not effect size (an n=3 signed-rank floors at p=0.125), which is why the headline significance is the pooled test plus the 54/54 win-count rather than per-dataset p-values. (ii) The boundary null is genuine: on Traffic (R=0.14) RR-MoA does not beat the best fixed adapter even under the most powerful uncorrected test (+2.9%, p=0.88). We will report uncorrected and Holm/BH values alongside Bonferroni.

**Q1 (apply raw routing to an existing MoE, e.g. AdaMix).** This is exactly the experiment we ran, and it is in the submission. AdaMix-Raw swaps only AdaMix's hidden-state router for a raw-input router, leaving its experts and training loop unchanged: routing entropy recovers from near-collapse to near-uniform (0.49→1.55) and MSE improves 42-88% across all 12 cells. This isolates the router input as the dominant cause, exactly the clarification requested.

**Q2 (generality to image / other domains).** App. H.1 replicates the mechanism in vision: a ViT-B/16 with InstanceNorm1d on patch embeddings collapses (0.000), while ResNet-18 does not (its InstanceNorm2d strips statistics that do not carry the routing signal), matching the theory across modality.

**On significance.** We are glad the review already recognizes the signal-ratio's value ("predicting dataset vulnerability prior to training... practical value beyond the specific method"). We would stress that this is the paper's most transferable contribution: R(D) is training-free and computed a priori (ρ=-0.88, n=9), so a practitioner can decide before any training whether raw routing will help on a new dataset, and it correctly abstains where it will not (the Traffic null, R=0.14). Together with the collapse diagnosis it explains, that is a reusable result for the TSFM-adapter community, independent of which backbone is best on a given dataset. And the diagnosis is general, not LTSF-specific: it reproduces under BatchNorm and GroupNorm and in a vision modality (App. H, H.1), so the significance extends to any instance-normalized backbone paired with a mixture head.

Given that the memory, expert-scaling, normalizer-generality, task-diversity, convergence, and statistical concerns are now addressed, several of them from evidence already in the submission, we would be grateful if the reviewer would consider re-evaluating the score.
