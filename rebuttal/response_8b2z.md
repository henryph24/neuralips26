We address each point in turn.

**W1 (memory of raw routing).** We can see why "routing on raw inputs" sounds memory-heavy; in practice the footprint is small. The router reads a single univariate length-L window and is an 853-parameter Conv1d gate (0.002% of the ~40M backbone, 57× smaller than a 49K DLinear), adding under 0.1 ms to the backbone's 52.9 ms forward pass. Total overhead across all three variants is +5.9% latency and +6 MB on a 359 MB backbone (Table B.1); peak memory is set by the frozen backbone every adapter method already loads.

**W2 (dataset-distributional / feature-asymmetry factors).** We appreciate this point, and you are right that the formal theory is intentionally narrow. Observation 1 isolates the single removable quantity, per-sample location-scale, and that narrowness is exactly what makes the result usable before training: R(D) is a training-free predictor of which datasets will collapse (ρ=-0.88, n=9), and it correctly abstains on the boundary cases (Traffic R=0.14, Solar R=0.06). We then broaden the account in two ways. First, Appendix D.1 recasts the same loss through two standard information-theoretic views. The rate-distortion view treats instance normalization as lossy compression that deliberately discards each window's mean and scale, so R(D) measures how much of the routing signal lives in exactly that discarded part, rather than being a hand-built ratio. The information-bottleneck view explains why collapse happens at all: the normalize-then-encode pipeline is trained to keep only what predicts the target and drop the rest, and "the rest" includes what the router needs. Second, the mechanism holds empirically well beyond the instance-normalization/router pair: it reproduces under BatchNorm1d and GroupNorm and in a vision modality (App. H, H.1, and W4 below). What we do not yet formalize is dataset-specific distributional structure and feature asymmetries beyond mean and scale; we see that as genuine open theory, and our own ablations already show that what the router relies on is broader than mean and standard deviation (μ,σ) (App. F, SSR +48%).

**W3 (scaling experts → router-input dimensionality / bandwidth).** This is a natural worry, since in a standard MoE the router input does grow with the expert count. In our design it does not: The router input is the raw window and is independent of K; only the K-way logit layer (64→K) grows, which is negligible. Because routing is Top-2, exactly two experts run per sample regardless of K, so both active compute and memory traffic stay at O(2), not O(K). Table I.3 (K∈{1,2,3,5,7,10}) confirms this, with performance stable for K≥2. So the expert count creates no router-input or bandwidth bottleneck. [NEW] Extending the published sweep to K=15 and K=20 (ETTh1, 3 seeds), MSE stays stable (0.67-0.73), routing entropy stays at 91-97% of its log K ceiling (no collapse), and RR-MoA still beats the best fixed adapter by ~40% at every K. Beyond pool size, we varied the pool composition (richer conv/residual/gated and deeper MLP experts, a ten-way pool) and took a first step on learned pools (a hypernetwork generates each expert). Both stay healthy and beat the best fixed adapter; the canonical five stay strongest, and a rigorous learned-pool method (larger generators, NAS search) is future work.

**W4 (other normalizers: BatchNorm / LayerNorm / RMSNorm).** This is a natural question, and the submitted App. H already tests it interventionally: swapping RevIN for BatchNorm1d (entropy 0.62→0.004) and GroupNorm (0.51→0.000) yields identical collapse, while removing normalization keeps it healthy (0.82). [NEW] To match this rigor for LayerNorm, we also swap a LayerNorm into MOMENT's input-normalization position (same App. H protocol): it collapses identically (entropy → 0.000), with the no-normalization control healthy at 0.825. The tension with our LayerNorm/RMSNorm negative controls (Chronos, Timer-XL) comes down to where the norm sits. At the input position, it strips per-window statistics and collapses. Encoder-internal LayerNorm/RMSNorm do not strip the router's input, so they do not collapse. The rule is whether the normalizer strips routing-relevant statistics at the router's input, so the causal mechanism is not specific to instance normalization.

**W5 (only forecasting / imputation).** Beyond forecasting on six datasets and imputation (Table J.1, 7/8 wins), [NEW] a classification mechanism-check on three UEA datasets shows routing entropy stays healthy (1.46-1.59, no collapse), so the anti-collapse mechanism is not forecasting-specific; we scope this to mechanism-generalization since frozen features cap absolute accuracy. [NEW] An input-length ablation (L∈{96,192,336,512}, 72 runs) shows no collapse at any L and raw beats normalized routing at all 24 cells. [NEW] A reconstruction-based anomaly-detection check on SMD adds a fourth task type: routing entropy stays healthy (1.52-1.61, no collapse), so the mechanism is not forecasting-specific, with absolute detection frozen-capped (ROC-AUC ~0.55).

**W6 / Q3 (few epochs / convergence).** The complex dual-stream Residual-IA+ reaches its best validation point early and overfits by 50 epochs; the simpler RR-MoA handles long training well. Extending full fine-tuning to 50 and 100 epochs (90-config sweep) still loses to 15-epoch frozen RR-MoA by 33-71% (App. O). [NEW] We train RR-MoA to 200 epochs on ETTh1, ETTm1, Weather and Electricity (the dataset flagged as slowest to converge; 3 seeds each): routing never collapses (seed-mean entropy 1.02-1.34, minimum 0.87 across all 12 runs), and MSE is stable-to-improved versus 15 epochs (ETTh1 0.680→0.646, ETTm1 0.564→0.493). The reported 15-epoch numbers are conservative, not a convergence artifact.

**W7 (statistical corrections may be too conservative).** You are right that an over-conservative correction risks false negatives; we address both directions. [NEW] (i) Our claimed effect is correction-immaterial: the pooled Wilcoxon signed-rank on the RR-MoA-vs-best-fixed cells gives p ≈ 3×10⁻¹¹ uncorrected, so Bonferroni over every comparison in the paper still leaves it below 10⁻⁹, and Holm/BH only lower it further. A per-dataset Wilcoxon with 3-7 seeds is limited by seed count, not by effect size: an n=3 signed-rank floors at p=0.125. That is why our headline significance is the pooled test plus the 54/54 win-count, not per-dataset p-values. (ii) The boundary null is genuine: on Traffic (R=0.14) RR-MoA does not beat the best fixed adapter even under the most powerful uncorrected test (+2.9%, p=0.88). We will report uncorrected and Holm/BH values alongside Bonferroni.

**Q1 (apply raw routing to an existing MoE, e.g. AdaMix).** We appreciate this suggestion, and it is exactly what our AdaMix-Raw experiment does. AdaMix-Raw swaps only AdaMix's hidden-state router for a raw-input router, leaving its experts and training loop unchanged: routing entropy recovers from near-collapse to near-uniform (0.49→1.55) and MSE improves 42-88% across all 12 cells (Table G.4). This isolates the router input as the dominant cause, exactly the clarification requested.

**Q2 (generality to image / other domains).** Yes, App. H.1 replicates the mechanism in vision: a ViT-B/16 with InstanceNorm1d on patch embeddings collapses (0.000), while ResNet-18 does not (its InstanceNorm2d strips statistics that do not carry the routing signal), matching the theory across modality.

**On significance.** We are glad the review already recognizes the signal-ratio's value ("predicting dataset vulnerability prior to training... practical value beyond the specific method"). We would stress that this is the paper's most transferable contribution: R(D) is training-free and computed a priori (ρ=-0.88, n=9), so a practitioner can decide before any training whether raw routing will help on a new dataset, and it correctly abstains where it will not (the Traffic null, R=0.14). Together with the collapse diagnosis it explains, that is a reusable result for the TSFM-adapter community, independent of which backbone is best on a given dataset. And the diagnosis is general, not LTSF-specific: it reproduces under BatchNorm and GroupNorm and in a vision modality (App. H, H.1), so the significance extends to any instance-normalized backbone paired with a mixture head.

Given that the memory, expert-scaling, normalizer-generality, task-diversity, convergence, and statistical concerns are now addressed, several of them from evidence already in the submission, we would be grateful if the reviewer would consider re-evaluating the score.
