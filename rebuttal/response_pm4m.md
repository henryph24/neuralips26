**W3 (Moirai gains without collapse).** The reviewer is right, and we should have made this clearer. On Moirai there is no collapse, so its 12-29% gain is not collapse-repair. We read it as two results. (1) It confirms the prediction: Moirai has no learnable-affine input normalization, so the co-adaptation that drives collapse cannot occur. (2) Raw routing still helps, for a different reason: Moirai's non-learnable normalization (Kim et al. 2022) also strips per-window location-scale from the hidden states, and the raw input lets the router recover it, a routing effect, not the raw-signal predictiveness the reviewer links to App. P.3: in RR-MoA the raw signal feeds only the router, and experts use frozen hidden states. So the gain cannot come from the Pure Raw-MLP mechanism, where experts read the raw signal directly. So the cross-backbone wins split into two regimes the diagnosis predicts: collapse-repair on learnable-affine RevIN backbones, routing-signal recovery elsewhere. That is a sharper claim than uniform collapse-repair, not a weaker one; the causal chain stands, and we will add the Moirai citation.

**W5 ((μ,σ) tension).** *(Meta-review point 2: "the (M,Σ)-centric quantity contradicts with paper's ablations.")* We understand why this looks like a contradiction; it dissolves once we separate what the router reads from what normalization removes. Normalization is by definition a per-window location-scale map, so it removes exactly (M,Σ); Observation 1 and R(D) are therefore (M,Σ)-centric by necessity, and never claim (M,Σ) is the whole routing signal. R(D) is a ratio: routing signal in the removed part (location-scale) over routing signal in the surviving part (shape). The reviewer's two ablations are exactly that denominator: [μ,σ]-only routing degrades +48% because it drops the surviving shape, and temporal shuffling barely moves performance (≤3% on 4/6, the rest improve) because the signal is order-invariant shape, what normalization leaves. Both populate the denominator, not contradict the numerator. So the theory matches the data: collapse at high R(D), none at low (Traffic R=0.14, where RR-MoA correctly does not improve). We will re-scope Observation 1 as a lower-bound vulnerability predictor, not a claim that (μ,σ) alone is enough.
