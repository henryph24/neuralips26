**W3 (Moirai gains without collapse).** The reviewer is right, and we should have made this distinction clearly. On Moirai there is no collapse, so its 12-29% gain is not collapse-repair. We read Moirai as two separate results. (1) It confirms what the mechanism predicts: Moirai has no learnable-affine input normalization, so the gradient co-adaptation that drives collapse cannot occur, and Moirai does not collapse. (2) Raw routing still helps, but for a different reason. Moirai's non-learnable instance normalization (Kim et al. 2022) also strips per-window location-scale from the hidden states, and the raw input lets the router recover it. This is a routing effect, not the raw-signal predictiveness the reviewer links to App. P.3. The reason is the architecture: in RR-MoA the raw signal feeds only the router, and the experts use frozen hidden states, never the raw input. So the Moirai gain cannot come from the Pure Raw-MLP mechanism, where the experts read the raw signal directly. We therefore agree the cross-backbone wins should be shown as two regimes: collapse-repair on learnable-affine RevIN backbones, and routing-signal recovery elsewhere. They are not uniform proof of collapse. We will add the Moirai citation.

**W4 (heavy tuning; generalization beyond six).** On "heavy tuning": the 546-run sweep the reviewer cites (reported in Appendix P) was a one-time search to find the recipe, not a cost repeated per dataset. The recipe is then fixed. We agree it should not be presented as a SOTA search. The result, though, is a win over DLinear (65:11), not mere parity. [NEW] Applied unchanged (gate-init b=-2, 5-epoch warmup, shared NLinear, validation early-stop) to three datasets it had never seen, Residual-IA+ matches or beats DLinear on 8/9 (dataset, seed) cells: Solar 3/3 (-9.5%), Traffic 3/3 (parity), Exchange 2/3, no collapse. A recipe that transfers to unseen data without re-tuning is hard to see as dataset-specific engineering, and it speaks directly to "generalization beyond these six is unclear."

On "the raw branch does the work; the TSFM is dead weight (App. P.3)": one clarification we should have made explicit. P.3's "dead weight" conclusion is measured against the intermediate Dual-Stream (ETTh2 0.452, ETTm2 0.252), not the final Residual-IA+ (ETTh2 0.346, ETTm2 0.188), and that intermediate architecture itself loses to DLinear. P.3's own backbone-free Raw-MLP MoE loses to DLinear by +11.2% on all six datasets: remove the TSFM and you fall behind DLinear; it is the TSFM-inclusive Residual-IA+ that gets ahead. [NEW] We ran the clean test directly: Residual-IA+ vs a same-size backbone-free Raw-MLP MoE, with the raw branch held fixed so only the backbone differs, at H=192 (where the paper says the backbone helps most). Residual-IA+ wins 18/18 (mean -11.8%), including ETTh2/ETTm2/Electricity, the very datasets called dead-weight at H=96, and it still matches or beats a 49K DLinear. The backbone's contribution is therefore horizon-dependent: small at H=96, decisively positive at H≥192.

**W5 ((μ,σ) tension).** We understand why this looks like a contradiction, and re-scoping Observation 1 resolves it. R(D) is a ratio. The top is the routing signal in the part that normalization removes (location-scale); the bottom is the routing signal in the part that survives (shape). R(D) is not a claim that (μ,σ) is the whole routing signal. When the ratio is high, stripping (μ,σ) removes most of the routing signal and routing collapses; when it is low (Traffic, R=0.14) the signal lives in the surviving shape, so stripping does not hurt and RR-MoA correctly does not improve. This is exactly consistent with the ablations cited: [μ,σ]-only routing degrades +48% (SSR) and temporal shuffling moves performance ≤3%. We agree the real routing signal is broader than (μ,σ): the order-invariant shape that survives normalization is precisely R(D)'s surviving component. We will re-scope Observation 1 explicitly as a vulnerability predictor / lower bound, not a claim that (μ,σ) alone is enough.

**On significance.** The lasting contribution is the diagnosis and its predictor, and the reframing above sharpens rather than shrinks it. R(D) turns that diagnosis into a practical, training-free tool: computed from raw statistics before any training, it predicts whether raw routing will help on a new dataset (ρ=-0.88 across nine datasets), and equally where it will not (the Traffic null, R=0.14, predicted in advance and confirmed). This test transfers beyond our six benchmarks and beyond our specific router, since two architecturally distinct variants (SR-MoA and Residual-IA+) confirm it. The diagnosis, the R(D) predictor, and the raw-routing intervention are the contribution we ask the reader to take from the paper.
