**W3 (Moirai gains without collapse).** A fair distinction. Moirai applies non-learnable instance normalization (Kim et al. 2022, i.e. RevIN) to inputs/outputs, stripping the same per-window location-scale we study, minus the learnable affine. Raw routing recovers the stripped signal there by the same input-side mechanism, while the absence of learnable affine is exactly why AdaMix does not collapse on Moirai: the sudden collapse is driven by gradient co-adaptation and requires a learnable affine. The unified claim is "raw routing helps whenever per-window statistics are stripped from the router's input," and collapse is the severe failure mode that additionally requires learnable affine. We will present the cross-backbone results as consistent with the diagnosis rather than as uniform collapse-repair, and add the Moirai citation.

**W5 ((μ,σ) tension).** The tension is real, and re-scoping Observation 1 resolves it: R(D) is the ratio of routing-discriminative variance in the instance-norm-removable component (location-scale) to that in the surviving component (shape), not a claim that (μ,σ) is the whole routing signal. When the ratio is high, stripping (μ,σ) removes most of the routing signal and routing collapses; when it is low (Traffic, R=0.14) the signal lives in the surviving shape, so stripping does not hurt and RR-MoA correctly does not improve. This is exactly consistent with the ablations cited: [μ,σ]-only routing degrades +48% (SSR) and temporal shuffling moves performance ≤3%. We will re-scope Observation 1 explicitly as a vulnerability predictor / lower bound, not a sufficiency claim.

**On significance.** The durable contribution is the diagnosis and its predictor, and the reframing above sharpens rather than shrinks it. Normalization-induced routing collapse is a real, mechanistically-explained failure of the standard MoE-adapter upgrade on the dominant instance-normalized TSFM class. R(D) turns that diagnosis into an actionable, training-free tool: computed from raw statistics before any training, it predicts whether raw routing will help on a new dataset (ρ=-0.88 across nine datasets), and equally where it will not (the Traffic null, R=0.14, predicted a priori and confirmed). This go/no-go test transfers beyond our six benchmarks and beyond our specific router, since two architecturally distinct variants (SR-MoA and Residual-IA+) confirm the principle, and it holds regardless of which backbone is best on a given dataset.
