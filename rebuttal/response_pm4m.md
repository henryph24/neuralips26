**W1 (framing / DLinear).** This is the central point, and we treat it as such. The reviewer is correct that our abstract's headline numbers (54/54; 12-79% over full fine-tuning) are all comparisons within the frozen-adapter/PEFT regime. Those are the right baselines for the frozen-adapter question we ask, and we agree a from-scratch DLinear belongs beside them as a benchmark from outside that regime. Against that benchmark, the reviewer's point holds for RR-MoA-proper, the minimal diagnostic probe whose only role is to isolate the router input. It does not, however, apply to the method we deploy: Residual-IA+, reported in the submitted Appendix P, matches or beats DLinear rather than trailing it:

- 65 significant wins vs 11 losses (5.9:1); 107/123 match-or-beat (87%) across 6 backbones × 6 datasets × 4 horizons.
- 6/6 at the H=192 deployment horizon; MOMENT-large 23/24 with zero losses (16 significant wins); Moirai-MoE 15/15 (100%).

The criticism is fair about our framing, not our evidence. We led with "5/6 match-or-beat" and titled the appendix "Closing the DLinear Gap," which highlighted the single losing cell (ETTh1/H=96). We will fix the presentation in three ways:

- Reframe the abstract's DLinear comparison as a matter of scope, not a weakness, with wording such as: "Because the router isolates the collapse mechanism rather than maximizing accuracy, the minimal RR-MoA probe trails a from-scratch DLinear on linear-dominated datasets; our deployment adapter Residual-IA+ removes this gap, matching or beating DLinear across six backbones and four horizons."

We will present RR-MoA-proper plainly as a diagnostic tool, not an accuracy claim.

**W2 (Figure 2).** Agreed. We will redraw Figure 2 at larger size with non-overlapping modules so it clearly depicts both the raw-router and frozen-expert paths and the paper's core contribution, raw-input routing as the causal fix.

**W3 (Moirai gains without collapse).** The reviewer is right, and we should have made this distinction clearly: on Moirai there is no collapse, so its 12-29% gain is not collapse-repair. We read Moirai as two separate results. (1) It confirms the mechanism's negative prediction: with no learnable-affine input normalization, the gradient co-adaptation that drives collapse cannot occur, and Moirai does not collapse, exactly as the diagnosis requires. (2) Raw routing still helps because Moirai's non-learnable instance normalization (Kim et al. 2022) strips per-window location-scale from the hidden states, so restoring the raw input lets the router recover it. This is a routing effect, not the raw signal's predictive value inside the experts, which the reviewer links to App. P.3: in RR-MoA the raw signal feeds only the router, while the experts use frozen hidden states and never see the raw input, so the Moirai gain cannot be the Pure Raw-MLP mechanism, whose experts use the raw signal directly. We therefore agree the cross-backbone wins should be presented as two regimes, collapse-repair on learnable-affine RevIN backbones and routing-signal recovery elsewhere, not as uniform validation of the collapse diagnosis, and we will add the Moirai citation.

**W4 (heavy tuning; generalization beyond six).** On "heavy tuning": the 546-run sweep the reviewer cites (reported in Appendix P) was a one-time search to find the recipe, not a cost repeated per dataset. The recipe is then fixed. We agree it should not be presented as a SOTA search. The result, though, is a win over DLinear (65:11), not mere parity. [NEW] Applied unchanged (gate-init b=-2, 5-epoch warmup, shared NLinear, validation early-stop) to three datasets it had never seen, Residual-IA+ matches or beats DLinear on 8/9 (dataset, seed) cells: Solar 3/3 (-9.5%), Traffic 3/3 (parity), Exchange 2/3, no collapse. A recipe that transfers to unseen data without re-tuning argues against the view that it is dataset-specific engineering, and directly addresses "generalization beyond these six is unclear."

On "the raw branch does the work; the TSFM is dead weight (App. P.3)": one clarification we should have made explicit. P.3's "dead weight" conclusion is measured against the intermediate Dual-Stream (ETTh2 0.452, ETTm2 0.252), not the final Residual-IA+ (ETTh2 0.346, ETTm2 0.188), and that intermediate architecture itself loses to DLinear. P.3's own backbone-free Raw-MLP MoE loses to DLinear by +11.2% on all six datasets: remove the TSFM and you fall behind DLinear; it is the TSFM-inclusive Residual-IA+ that gets ahead. [NEW] We ran the clean isolation directly, Residual-IA+ vs a same-size backbone-free Raw-MLP MoE, holding the raw branch fixed and differing only in the backbone, at H=192 (where the paper reports the backbone contributes maximally): Residual-IA+ wins 18/18 (mean -11.8%), including ETTh2/ETTm2/Electricity, the very datasets flagged as dead-weight at H=96, while still matching or beating a 49K DLinear. The backbone's contribution is therefore horizon-dependent: small at H=96 on linear-dominated data, decisively positive at H≥192.

**W5 ((μ,σ) tension).** The tension is real, and re-scoping Observation 1 resolves it: R(D) is the ratio of routing-discriminative variance in the instance-norm-removable component (location-scale) to that in the surviving component (shape), not a claim that (μ,σ) is the whole routing signal. When the ratio is high, stripping (μ,σ) removes most of the routing signal and routing collapses; when it is low (Traffic, R=0.14) the signal lives in the surviving shape, so stripping does not hurt and RR-MoA correctly does not improve. This is exactly consistent with the ablations cited: [μ,σ]-only routing degrades +48% (SSR) and temporal shuffling moves performance ≤3%. We agree the real routing signal is broader than (μ,σ): the order-invariant shape that survives normalization is precisely R(D)'s surviving component. We will re-scope Observation 1 explicitly as a vulnerability predictor / lower bound, not a claim that (μ,σ) alone is enough.

**On significance.** The lasting contribution is the diagnosis and its predictor, and the reframing above sharpens rather than shrinks it. Normalization-induced routing collapse is a real, mechanistically-explained failure mode. R(D) turns that diagnosis into a practical, training-free tool: computed from raw statistics before any training, it predicts whether raw routing will help on a new dataset (ρ=-0.88 across nine datasets), and equally where it will not (the Traffic null, R=0.14, predicted in advance and confirmed). This test transfers beyond our six benchmarks and beyond our specific router, since two architecturally distinct variants (SR-MoA and Residual-IA+) confirm it. The diagnosis, the R(D) predictor, and the raw-routing intervention are the contribution we ask the reader to take from the paper.

Given the corrected DLinear framing (Residual-IA+ outperforms DLinear 65:11, already in App. P), the re-scoped R(D) explanation, and the added input-length ablation, together with the reviewer's kind indication that addressing the concerns would raise the score, we would be grateful for a reconsideration of the rating.
